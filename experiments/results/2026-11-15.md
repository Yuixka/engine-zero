# First held-out trajectory benchmark — 2026-11-15

Executed locally with Node.js 22.16.0 using identical git blob hashes for graph-engine.js, legacy-fourbar.js and discovery.js as GitHub main at the time of the test.

```sh
node test.js
node experiments/test-benchmark.js
node experiments/benchmark.js --seed=716 --budget=120 --depth=2 --output=experiments/results/latest.json
```

- 121 attempts (including one imported four-bar incumbent); 121 passed solver checks; 5 family champions; 32 distinct labeled wiring signatures.
- The training objective is a synthetic step trajectory with 120 mm stroke and 38 mm lift, sampled at 36 positions.
- All evaluations below use 96 positions. Each family champion was frozen from training **before** held-out tests.

| Held-out target | Optimal circle baseline RMS | 4R RMS | 8-body RMS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Same step, denser sampling | 16.482 mm | 5.576 mm | 22.814 mm |
| Different gait (“quick”) | 19.128 mm | 16.435 mm | 25.470 mm |
| Different step stroke/lift | 18.006 mm | 8.468 mm | 26.407 mm |
| Oval trajectory family | 20.500 mm | 28.841 mm | 15.598 mm |

**Important:** The four-bar includes a previously optimized incumbent hardcoded in discovery.js. The data **do not** show that this run invented it, or that the eight-body machine is superior in general. The generator did not optimize each family equally. No force, friction, clearance, endurance, novelty, manufacturability or hardware validation is performed. Some held-out results are worse than a single-crank baseline. Follow-up: implement an independently timed/controlled cross-topology optimization budget and multiple seeds.
